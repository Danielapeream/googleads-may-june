04 · Root cause

Landing pages are killing two channels at once.

The pattern across keyword reports is unambiguous. Keywords pointing to /content/[carrier-name] pages with real content (Day Ross, Manitoulin) convert. Keywords pointing to empty or generic pages get an explicit flag from Google: "Eligible (limited) — Rarely shown (low quality score)".

Example found during the audit
https://www.freightsimple.com/content/shipping-companies-toronto/
What the user finds

Standard FreightSimple page. The header reads "Top Toronto Shipping Companies" but the content doesn't address the query. No actual listing, no comparison, no information that justifies the search.

Measurable impact

High bounce → Quality Score collapses → CPC inflates artificially → impressions lost in lower positions → organic SEO also fails because there's no useful content to index.

Evidence in Google Ads' own data

The keyword report for Day Ross shows exactly the pattern:

The math hurts

Keywords flagged as low quality accumulate 172 clicks with only 2 conversions (1.15%). Keywords with healthy Quality Score accumulate 41 clicks with 4 conversions (9.76%). Same campaign, same carrier, same budget — the only variable that changes is the quality of the landing page. It's ~8× more efficient when the LP works.

Why this problem is doubly expensive

The same defect attacks two channels at once:

In SEM (Google Ads): low Quality Score → inflated CPC → fewer impressions per dollar spent → conversions that would have happened cost more.

In organic SEO: thin or non-existent content → Google doesn't rank those URLs → organic traffic for queries like "ABF freight Canada" goes to competitors → eternal dependency on SEM because organic never takes off.

Fixing landing pages isn't an isolated SEO or SEM task — it's one lever that moves both channels at once.

05 · Risk assessment

The May 25 changes need to be revisited.

Three simultaneous campaign modifications were made within a 90-second window. Each individually is defensible; together they create attribution problems and a specific technical risk.

The technical problem with "Maximize Conversion Value"

Google's documentation is explicit: value-based Smart Bidding strategies require a minimum of 30–50 conversions per month for the model to learn how to optimize. Below that threshold, the algorithm:

· Overbids on clicks it believes are high-value (based on small, non-representative samples)
· Underbids on valuable clicks it hasn't yet learned to identify
· Generates high week-to-week volatility, making real performance hard to read

Conversion volume vs. Smart Bidding minimum threshold

Why three simultaneous changes are a methodological problem

If performance improves over the next two weeks, we won't know whether it was the budget increase, the new bid strategy, or disabling search partners. If performance drops, equally blind. This isn't a theoretical concern: any conclusions drawn in June will be hunches, not learnings.

Technical recommendation

Temporarily revert "Maximize Conversion Value" to "Maximize Conversions" (much lower threshold, ~15 conv/month) or manual CPC while scaling volume. Return to the value-based strategy once we hit 40+ conv/month consistently, not before.
